# Editorial Standards

The purpose of `country-guides` is to provide practical, structured, and maintainable travel information. Guides should help readers understand a destination without overwhelming them or pretending to answer every possible question.

## Voice

Use a calm, neutral, and practical voice. The writing should be useful to travelers, but it should not sound promotional, sensational, or overly casual.

Good guide writing explains tradeoffs. It does not declare that one destination, neighborhood, or travel style is universally best.

## Structure

Every guide should follow the relevant template. Consistent structure makes the repository easier to maintain and easier to reuse in static sites, knowledge bases, and downstream tools.

Country guides should use the country template. City guides should use the city template.

## Accuracy

Travel information changes. Avoid phrasing that implies permanent certainty when the detail may change.

Prefer wording such as:

- Travelers should verify current entry requirements before departure.
- Transit routes and fares may change.
- Seasonal conditions vary by region and year.
- Local rules should be checked through official sources.

Avoid wording such as:

- Visitors never need to worry about safety.
- This is always the cheapest option.
- The visa process is simple for everyone.
- This neighborhood is completely safe.

## Source hierarchy

Use official sources for high-stakes information. This includes immigration, public health, travel advisories, transport agencies, and emergency services.

Use reputable non-official sources for practical context, but do not treat them as final authorities.

## Safety language

Safety sections should be grounded and practical. They should not exaggerate risks, minimize risks, or rely on stereotypes.

Mention common travel risks in a measured way. Good safety guidance helps readers plan without creating unnecessary fear.

## Cultural respect

Guides should describe cultural norms respectfully. Avoid framing local customs as strange, backward, inconvenient, or inferior.

Where possible, explain why a norm matters and how travelers can act respectfully.

## Accessibility

Accessibility information should be included even when details are limited. It is better to note likely planning considerations than to ignore accessibility entirely.

Do not claim a destination is accessible unless the guide explains what that means.

## Prices and budgets

Avoid exact prices unless they are durable, sourced, and clearly dated. Use general language for cost patterns.

Examples:

- Accommodation is often more expensive in central areas and during major events.
- Rail passes may be useful for some itineraries, but point-to-point tickets can be better for shorter trips.
- Card payments are common, but carrying a small amount of cash may still be useful.

## Affiliate links and promotion

Do not include affiliate links, sponsored placements, or promotional recommendations.

Guides may mention categories of services, such as official transit apps, tourism offices, museums, coworking spaces, or booking platforms, but should avoid turning guides into commercial lists.
